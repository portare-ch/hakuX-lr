# NV2A accuracy: missing and inaccurate features

Source audit of the HLE Vulkan renderer (`hw/xbox/hle/`), dated 2026-10-29
at `532c8f9700`. Rows prioritize visible improvements with low GPU cost.
Cost estimates assume the Nova's Adreno 740, Turnip and 2x render scale.

The baseline column and line numbers refer to that commit; Status records
later changes. Visibility is estimated from code. In-game checks on the
Nova are noted explicitly.

| # | NV2A feature | Audit baseline | Visibility | GPU cost | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Anisotropic filtering** | Read from the register (`pb/kelvin.c:167`), but the sampler never turns it on (`vk/draw.c:1644`). `caps.aniso` is queried in `vkdev.c:587` and never used. | Med–High: floors and roads blur at angles in games that ask for it | Low, and only on stages the game sets | PR #28. Vulkan's anisotropic filtering is the driver's, so this approximates the NV2A's until compared with Xbox output, nearest filtering especially. |
| 2 | **Two-sided lighting / back colours** | Method `0x17C4` is never read. The vertex shaders write `vBack0`/`vBack1` (`shader/vsh.c:222`), but no fragment shader reads them. Fixed-function lighting doesn't light back faces (`shader/ffhw.c:202`). | Med: lit geometry drawn with culling off (foliage, cloth, hair, flags) shows front colours on its back faces | ~0 (pick by `gl_FrontFacing`) | PR #29 |
| 3 | **Texture formats L16, V16U16, R6G5B5 (L6V5U5), R5G5B5A1, R4G4B4A4**, and depth formats used as ordinary textures | No Vulkan mapping (`vk/draw.c:235`), so the texture is never uploaded (`vk/draw.c:975`). The draw still runs: `stage_slot()` falls back to slot 0, the white texture, so the stage samples white instead of the texture. | High when a game uses one (wrong or missing texturing on what it draws), but rare | 0 (converted at upload) | PR #35. R4G4B4A4 samples Vulkan's R4G4B4A4 (same layout, no swizzle: B4G4R4A4 swizzled would leave the format-less custom border colours undefined), else it is converted like R5G5B5A1; L16 is read as (1, high byte, low byte, 1), as an Xbox draws nxdk_pgraph_tests' Y16 test (and xemu reads it), not as luminance; R5G5B5A1, V16U16 (u, v, u, v, as G8B8) and D16 sample their own formats where the GPU has them, else they are converted on the way into the arena (`tex/xdk_res.c` `hle_tex_convert`); R6G5B5 is always expanded to A8R8G8B8. Depth textures from guest memory are compared like depth surfaces (`shadow_fmt`): D24S8, D16 and F16 scaled to 0..1 over their ranges, F24 as its float, r cut to its 16-bit mantissa (the test compares at 0.5e30). Not yet compared with an Xbox: V16U16's channels, L6V5U5's signed U and V (expanded like unsigned ones, so bump offsets read them as signed bytes); fixed-function draws still sample depth textures white. nxdk_pgraph_tests can't check more yet: hakuX skips its draws, whose surface addresses are in DMA objects (offsets 0). |
| 4 | **Texture colour sign** (`TSS_COLORSIGN`) | Stored (`kelvin.c:174`), only logged | Med in games with signed normal maps read straight by the combiners: bump lighting comes out flat or inverted | ~0 | PR #30 |
| 5 | **Colour key** (`COLORKEYOP`) | Stored (`kelvin.c:172`), only logged. The key-colour methods are never read. The four modes act on a texel that matches the key: 0 no change, 1 its alpha zeroed, 2 its ARGB zeroed, 3 the fragment killed. Only mode 3 may discard; modes 1 and 2 change the texel and later combiner stages can ignore or replace those channels. | Low–Med: black or magenta boxes around sprites in games that use it | ~0 (a compare per keyed stage; mode 3 discards, which turns off early depth rejection for those draws) | PR #31 |
| 6 | **Quincunx / Gaussian blur filters** | Treated as plain bilinear (`kelvin.c:161`) | Med: games that smooth their antialiasing with Quincunx/Gaussian look sharper and more jagged than on an Xbox; blur effects come out weaker | Low (usually one full-screen pass). Make it optional, since some players prefer the sharper image. | PR #32 |
| 7 | **Line width** (`0x0380`) | Always 1.0 (`draw.c:2100`) and not scaled by render scale. Points are scaled (`vsh.c:336`). `caps.wide_lines` is never used. | Low–Med: at the 2x default, lines (HUDs, radars, beams) are half as thick as on hardware | ~0 | PR #33 |
| 8 | **Dithering** (`0x0310`) | Never read. 16-bit render targets stay 16-bit (`surf.c:121`) and band. | Low–Med: banded skies, fog and gradients in games that draw into 16-bit targets | ~0 (ordered dither in the shader) | PR #34 |
| 9 | **Unsupported pixel-shader modes**: depth replace (`DOT_ZW`), `BRDF`, `DOT_RFLCT_SPEC_CONST` | Fall back to a plain 2D texture lookup (`psh.c:504`). `SPEC_CONST` uses the per-pixel eye vector, not a constant one (`psh.c:495`). | Low (wrong but not missing) | Low–Med: depth replace writes the depth in the shader, which turns off early depth rejection for those draws | PR #42. `DOT_ZW` (stage 2 or 3) replaces the fragment's depth with the previous stage's dot over its own (NV_texture_shader's DOT_PRODUCT_DEPTH_REPLACE), in depth-buffer units as a program's screen z is (nxdk_pgraph_tests' DotZW makes 10000 and more) and normalized the same way; w 0 is the far plane (D3D's texm3x2depth), and the fragment is rejected outside the buffer's range, clamped with ZCLAMP_EN. It replaces W-buffering's depth too, and the EQUAL-depth tolerance test reads it. The stage's colour is 0. `DOT_RFLCT_SPEC_CONST` reflects about `SET_EYE_VECTOR` (0x181C, envytools' TEX_SHADER_CONST_EYE; (0, 0, -1) until set), carried in stage 3's own bump-matrix push constants. `BRDF` (stage 2 or 3) looks its volume up at (eye elevation, light elevation, light minus eye azimuth) from stages n-2 and n-1 (NVIDIA's patent US6664963), each a pair of 16-bit numbers; elevation is taken from the high half, azimuth from the low (g and r, V16U16's V and U, as Cxbx-Reloaded reads them). Without a volume it reads the 2D texture at the two elevations. Not compared with an Xbox: DotZW's depth buffer against its capture, the clip range (hakuX keeps the buffer's whole range), the BRDF halves' order and sign, the eye vector's power-on value. Not checked in a game. |
| 10 | **Logic ops** (`0x17BC` / `0x17C0`) | Never read. The device feature is enabled (`vkdev.c:686`) but unused. | Low | ~0 | Open |
| 11 | **Window clip** | Exclusive mode is ignored, and several inclusive rectangles become one bounding box (`pbdraw.c:932`) | Low: split-screen, picture-in-picture, HUD masks | Low | PR #40. The clip as disjoint rectangles inside the surface clip (`pb/wclip.c`): the union of the inclusive windows, or everything outside the exclusive ones. The back end records a draw once in each, scissored to it, so one inclusive window stays a plain scissor and costs nothing; one exclusive window draws up to 4 times (its vertices each time, its fragments once). Not compared with an Xbox: a window whose two words are 0 counts as unused (D3D leaves the ones it doesn't set so), where the registers read literally make it one pixel at the origin. |
| 12 | **Wireframe / point fill modes, smooth lines and polygons** (`0x038C`, `0x0390`, `0x0320`, `0x0324`) | Always solid fill | Low | Low | PR #108. Supports front/back fill modes, line width, point size and mode-specific polygon offset. Different face modes use two passes, which may change draw order. Line smoothing uses Vulkan line rasterization; polygon smoothing uses barycentric coverage before alpha testing. Both require multisampling off and fall back when GPU features are missing. Cached pipelines respect available features. Limitations: internal wireframe diagonals, ignored edge flags and interior-only smoothing. Tenchu: Return from Darkness uses polygon smoothing. Debug: `HAKUX_HLE_DBGFLAGS=64/128` forces lines/points (`+256`: back faces only). |
| 13 | **Antialiasing control** (`0x1D7C`: sample mask, alpha-to-coverage) | Never read | Low, though a game relying on alpha-to-coverage would show hard edges | Low–Med, because antialiased surfaces are emulated as larger images | PR #38. Read into the draw state (`aactl`, D3D's 0xFFFF0001 until set; the bits as NV30's MULTISAMPLE_CONTROL: 0 multisampling, 4 alpha-to-coverage, 8 alpha-to-one, 31:16 the sample mask). On an antialiased target with multisampling on, each pixel of the larger image is a sample of its guest pixel: the pixel shader discards one the mask leaves out or alpha-to-coverage doesn't cover (the first alpha x N), then alpha-to-one sets alpha to 1. Not compared with an Xbox: the sample order (row-major assumed) and the coverage pattern (in sample order, no dither across pixels); multisampling off still draws every sample on its own. Only draws using them pay: a discard, and a pipeline of their own. |
| 14 | **Spotlight falloff and specular curve** | Linear spotlight cone that ignores the falloff (`ffhw.c:157`); the specular exponent is reconstructed from the hardware's coefficients | Low | 0 | PR #41. Both are the lighting unit's curve, (x + L) / (M x + N), over the methods' coefficients (NVIDIA's patent US6417851), as xemu evaluates it after comparing with the hardware tests (nxdk_pgraph_tests' Specular and Lighting spotlight). The specular term uses the first three of `SET_SPECULAR_PARAMS` (and of `_BACK`); the other three (D3D's half power) stay unused, their use unknown. What a denominator of 0 or below gives, and the power-on values, are xemu's. Not checked in a game. |
| 15 | **Combiner precision** | Floats clamped to [-1, 1] per stage (`psh.c:156`); the NV2A uses roughly 9-bit fixed point | Low (subtle banding; rare effects that depend on rounding) | Low, but adds work to every combiner shader | PR #109. Accurate mode rounds stage outputs to signed 9-bit fixed point (1.0 = 0xFF); Fast mode keeps float stage outputs. The 0x80 bias and other combiner fixes apply in both modes. Accurate defaults on AMD64 Linux and Fast on ARM64/Android. All 116 Xbox combiner reference pixels match, up from 86. The final V1R0_SUM clamps its operands separately; signed combiner texels use /127.5, while texture-shader reads use /127. A small tie correction matches 8-bit inputs but can round finer-texture near-ties upward. In PGR2, the corrected MSB MUX makes GTO/Mini chrome brighter; real Xbox comparison is pending. Fixed-function stages still use float math. Cost: about three ALU operations per written register; Nova performance unmeasured. |
| 16 | **GL-style clamp** (address mode 5) | Treated as clamp-to-edge (`draw.c:1582`). The maximum-LOD clamp and the fractional part of the minimum LOD are dropped. | Very low | Low–Med | Open |
| 17 | **Cylindrical texture wrap** | The wrap bits are masked off (`kelvin.c:156`) | Low: seams on cylinder-mapped geometry | High: needs per-triangle work, which costs a lot on a tile-based GPU like the Adreno | PR #172. Off by default: core option `hakux_cylindrical_wrap`, `HAKUX_HLE_CYLWRAP=1`, Android `debug.hakux.cylwrap`. The wrap bits (`SET_TEXTURE_ADDRESS` bits 4, 12, 20, 24: s, t, r, q) reach the pixel-shader keys of the stages that use them. Such a stage reads its coordinates per vertex (`pervertexEXT`), moves vertices 1 and 2 by 1.0 on each wrapped axis where they are more than 0.5 from vertex 0, and interpolates with the perspective-correct barycentrics. No geometry shader: only wrapped stages pay, a few ALU operations each. Without fragment shader barycentrics the wrap is ignored. Point lists and point-sprite coordinates are not wrapped. The first draws that ask for it are logged (`cylindrical wrap`), also when it is off. Not compared with an Xbox: the reference vertex and the 0.5 threshold (D3D's shortest way, defined for coordinates in [0, 1]); coordinates outside [0, 1], moved by 1.0 at most; the q axis; lines; which texture modes it applies to (here every mode's coordinates). Not checked in a game. |

## To verify before changing

**Provoking vertex.** The Vulkan device enables "last vertex" for flat
shading (`vkdev.c:618`), but no pipeline selects it, so flat-shaded draws take
their colour from the first vertex. Which vertex the NV2A uses, especially
for quads split into triangles, is unconfirmed. Test a flat-shaded draw
before changing anything.

## Already handled

Register combiners, including the final combiner; most texture modes; shadow
maps with filtered edges; W-buffering; depth clamp; float depth; fog modes;
fixed-function transform and lighting with skinning and texture-coordinate
generation; point sprites; occlusion queries; signed blending; framebuffer
reads; the video overlay, with its colour key and scaler; palettes; texture
borders; and 2x / 2x2 antialiased surfaces.

## Suggested order

1. Measure before fixing. The renderer already logs unsupported formats
   ("has no Vulkan format", "not uploaded yet") and texture modes ("sampled
   as PROJECT2D"). A one-time log when a game sets a non-default value for
   two-sided lighting, dithering, logic op, fill mode, antialiasing control
   or colour key would show which rows come up in the test games.
2. Rows 1 and 2: small changes with almost no GPU cost.
3. Rows 4 and 5, together with the colour-sign and colour-key logging in
   `psframe_log` (`vk/draw.c`).
